9-Stage Receiver · RECEIVER PIPELINE

From antenna to decision, all nine stages are our own code.

The receiver isn't a black box. We built every one of the nine stages that turn an RF signal into a digital decision in our own Rust engine, and verified seven of them with quantitative benchmarks. You don't have to click anything. Scroll down and each stage's technology lights up in the order the signal actually flows through it.

Capability Deep-Dive · Axis 3, Signal Intelligence Engine

The medium changes. The math behind the judgment doesn't.

Whether it's an electromagnetic wave, sound, or the faint flicker of a WiFi channel, the question that keeps coming up on site always narrows down to one thing: is this signal different from what we already know, and how much can we trust that judgment? Three proven pieces of math answer that question: open-set fingerprint rejection, fixed-false-alarm-rate anomaly detection, and correlation-based fingerprint matching. We keep exactly these three and port them across media, from RF to audio, to vibration, to WiFi channel state. So we're less a company that invents a new algorithm for every industry, and more a company that manages how far one proven decision logic can reach across media.

RF is the medium where that logic accumulated first, and longest. We benchmarked the engine behind Case 06 (factory wireless interference diagnosis) on our own test suite. Instead of accuracy against a closed list, we measure head-on the ability to reject a signal source it has never seen (open-set), and it runs as-is on edge hardware and closed networks in the field, with no GPU.

But the 0.945 and 0.9994 figures above are under a same-session, multi-packet fusion condition, and under real product conditions (cross-day, 1:N-attacker), TAR@FAR at 1e-3 drops to a range of 0.22 to 0.43. Edge deployment that runs as-is in closed networks with no GPU is a strength that doesn't change, but we're telling you plainly that product-grade authentication performance is still under verification.

A New Sales Channel · Embedding in Equipment Vendors

This time we embed inside the equipment vendor, not the end user.

Everything covered so far was a picture of us selling directly to companies. But the principle that the medium changes while the math behind the judgment doesn't holds just as well when we change who we sell to. A manufacturer of routers, test equipment, or industrial wireless gear licenses our engine, embeds it in their own box, and builds a premium product line around that feature to sell at a higher price. We never meet the end user directly. We supply the manufacturer in the form of a module and a license. It isn't an unfamiliar picture. Similar detection features are already shipping commercially on carrier CPE abroad, at tens of millions of households, and test-equipment companies already sell interference detection as a paid option. We're not opening a new market; we're arriving late into one that's already proven.
