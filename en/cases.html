The 2i gauge method

We don’t guess. We measure.

Every project passes three gates. We only spend the next stage’s budget once a gate is cleared. That structure removes the biggest fear in adopting AI: "what if we spend the money and it doesn’t work?"

  1. 01
    Measure GateMEASURE · diagnose

    We pin the problem down in numbers. We measure the current value of the frontline KPI and the real state of your data, so there is a baseline for how much has to improve.

  2. 02
    Verify GateVERIFY · PoC

    We pick one point and actually measure before against after. If it misses the target delta here, we do not scale it. The large spend comes after proof.

  3. 03
    Operate GateOPERATE · build

    We extend only what was verified and hand it over so your team can run it themselves, with documentation and manuals, and we stay until it performs.

Signal Fingerprinting · Telecom Smart Factory · Wireless Infrastructure
Interference-source identification Unidentified every time · temporary fixes

Pinpointing, in real time, the 'unidentified radio signal' that keeps stopping your wireless network

A factory's wireless sensor network, AGVs, and RFID keep dropping out from unidentified radio interference, stopping the line, but no one can pin down whether the cause is a microwave, nearby equipment, or an outside unlicensed transmitter, so every incident just gets a temporary fix. The few minutes the line is down are pure production loss, and because the cause is unknown, it keeps happening again.

Insight

The conventional wisdom for identifying an interference source is 'collect a mountain of training data and train a classifier,' but that kind of labeled data doesn't exist on the factory floor. Matching a signal's 'fingerprint' (its occupancy pattern) in real time, without training, lets you pinpoint the cause without ever building a dataset.

2i’s approach

We use our sigprint engine, which captures anomalous signals in real time with CFAR detection, registers the waveforms of candidate interference sources in a signature DB, and pinpoints the cause through robust-hash fingerprint matching. It's designed for millisecond-level processing and operation in weak-signal environments. The same fingerprinting approach can also extend to acoustic and vibration anomaly detection for equipment. On our own benchmark (WiSig, same-session multi-packet fusion / circular condition), the same engine measured an open-set identification AUROC of 0.945, correctly rejecting signal sources it had never seen. We're upfront that this figure is a research-condition result, and that under real product conditions, cross-day and 1:N-attacker settings, TAR@FAR of 1e-3 drops to roughly 0.22-0.43.

  1. Measure GateWe compile wireless network failure and equipment stoppage history by time segment, collect on-site RF spectrum data, confirm the initial pattern of when and on which band interference recurs, and narrow the suspect equipment down to a candidate pool.
  2.   3. Operate GateWe integrate the verified signature DB and decision logic into the operating system so that candidate causes are automatically surfaced as alerts whenever interference occurs, and hand over a staff dashboard and a response manual.

Insight

Ground-truth masks don't exist on the actual research floor. But the signal that a result is wrong is already sitting inside the image: whether the mask has fractured into hundreds of pieces, whether it has holes punched through it, whether the boundary sits on the image's real contours, and whether the resulting volume makes physiological sense. Tuning the tool using only these ground-truth-free signals still raises real accuracy.

2i’s approach

We capture the axial, coronal, and sagittal views, compute six quality signals that require no ground truth, and diagnose the cause from them. If it doesn't improve, we roll it back. The source image is never modified by any patch, and we don't expose an arbitrary code-execution tool.

  1. Measure GateWe measure how much manual correction time the current segmentation work takes and how reproducible it is across operators, then calibrate the quality signals and tolerances against ground-truth masks to fit the research purpose.
  2. Verify GateWe run the closed loop on de-identified research data and measure the self-correction rate, volume-measurement reproducibility, and the drop in expert correction time. What's being evaluated is the tool's operating performance, not clinical performance.
  3. Operate GateWe connect the verified pipeline to imaging workstations and the learning-based segmentation model inside the research network, and hand over operating rules for de-identification, read-only access, and network isolation along with it.

Workforce Scheduling · Optimization Hospitals · Healthcare Facilities
Legacy model roster success rate No solution in 12 of 18 runs (66.7%, measured)

Instead of "there's no solution," a roster that shows exactly what to trade off

Building a ward's three-shift roster by hand takes several days every month. The scheduler has to satisfy legal working-hour limits, caps on consecutive shifts and consecutive days off, and a fair split of night and weekend burden, all at once, and when those constraints are wired into simple rule logic, the moment the staff count or the period gets even slightly tight, the system stops with "no solution." The person in charge then ends up filling the gaps by hand, breaking rules where necessary, and no record is kept of where or why that violation happened.
