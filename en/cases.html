The 2i Gauge Method

We don't guess. We measure.

Every project passes through three gates. We spend the budget for the next stage only after clearing the current one. It's a structure designed to remove the single biggest fear in adopting AI: "what if we spend the money and it doesn't work?"

  1. 01
    Measure GateMEASURE · Diagnosis

    We pin the problem down in numbers. We measure the current value (baseline) of on-site KPIs and the actual state of the data, to set a baseline for what to improve and by how much.

  2. 02
    Verify GateVERIFY · PoC

    We pick a single point and actually measure before → after. If it falls short of the target delta here, we don't scale it up. The real spending comes after verification.

  3. 03
    Operate GateOPERATE · Build

    We scale up only what's been verified and hand it over so the operations team can run it themselves. Along with documentation and manuals, we stay engaged until results show.

01
Management Visibility Manufacturing · Precision Parts
Time to see current status Half a day+

"What's today's inventory?" From half a day to 3 seconds

When data is scattered across dozens of departmental spreadsheets and individual staff PCs, it takes the CEO half a day just to get today's production and inventory numbers, because someone has to manually collect them. The bigger loss is the one you don't see: each department calculates the same metric differently, and when the person in charge leaves, nobody knows where that data comes from or how it's updated, putting business continuity itself at risk.

Insight

The problem isn't the lack of a dashboard, it's that the 'source and definition' of each number exists only inside people's heads. Before making the screen look nice, you first have to lock down in code where each metric comes from and how it's calculated.

2i's Approach

We set up an ETL pipeline that automatically collects data from scattered spreadsheets and internal systems and refines it into a standard schema, then separate an executive summary view from a detailed shop-floor view, unifying both around a single source of truth. The core isn't the screen, it's a 'data flow that doesn't break even when the person in charge changes.'

  1. Measure Gate · 2 weeksWe survey every department's data location, update cycle, owner, and bottlenecks, then narrow down to the 3-5 metrics the CEO wants to see daily and measure the baseline.
  2. Verify Gate · 4 weeksWe automatically collect and refine just 1-2 priority metrics to build a real-time prototype, and adjust the update cycle and exception handling as frontline staff use it in actual work. If the numbers don't check out here, we don't scale it up.
  3. Operate Gate · 6 weeksWe extend the verified approach to the remaining data sources and hand it over with data-flow documentation and an operations manual, so operations continue uninterrupted even when staff change.

02
Document Automation Wholesale · Distribution
Manual processing per document 5-10 min × hundreds of documents

Manual quote and order entry, staff should only review exceptions

Quotes, purchase orders, and tax invoices arrive from each business partner in different formats, fax, PDF, or photo, and staff have to check them by eye and manually enter them into the ERP. The smallest mismatch in item name, quantity, or unit price triggers repeated re-checking and re-entry, and during month-end or quarter-end surges, this manual delay becomes the bottleneck for the entire order process.

Insight

Attempts to read documents 100% automatically mostly fail. The real leverage is building a review queue that lets the machine pass through anything it's confident about and routes only ambiguous items to a person, so people spend their time on exception judgment, not full-volume data entry.

2i's Approach

We extract table and line-item structure through document parsing, then cross-check item codes and unit prices against predefined rules to automatically classify entries and flag anomalies. Only low-confidence items are surfaced on a review screen, so staff go from 'entering everything' to 'checking only the exceptions.'

  1. Measure GateWe collect a sample of real transaction documents, measure format diversity, per-staff entry time, and recurring error points, and set automation priorities.
  2. Verify GateWe pilot the item-extraction and validation logic on a single document type and measure recognition accuracy and how exceptions are handled.
  3. Operate GateWe extend the verified logic to the remaining document types, add a semi-automated review screen and ERP integration, and hand it over to operations.

03
Manufacturing Vision AI Manufacturing · Injection Molding
Defect judgment criteria Varies by person and shift

Every inspector judges differently, now the same eyes, 24 hours a day

When final quality inspection relies on an inspector's naked eye, the same defect gets judged differently depending on skill level, fatigue, and lighting. Surface bubble and flash-leak rates swing wildly from shift to shift, and 'what counts as a defect' exists only as tacit knowledge in people's heads, which makes both training new inspectors and reproducible quality control difficult. A single defect that slips through and comes back as a claim or a return costs far more than the inspection labor itself.

Insight

Most attempts to convert an entire line to AI at once stall because neither the data nor the budget can support it. The right answer is to first narrow the scope to just the 1-2 most frequent and most clear-cut defect types and verify them with a small dataset of a few hundred images. Start low-risk, and scale only what works.

2i's Approach

We train a defect-detection vision model on product surface images using a small dataset, and run it in 'shadow mode' alongside the existing line, without replacing it, comparing its output to visual inspection. This isn't about removing people, it's about adding a first-pass screener that never gets tired, 24 hours a day.

  1. Measure GateWe classify per-inspector judgment variance and recurring defect types through an on-site walkthrough, and document 'what counts as a defect' through interviews.
  2. Verify GateWe photograph and label hundreds to thousands of images of the narrowed 1-2 defect types to train the model, then run it in shadow mode and produce a measured report on accuracy, false positives, and missed defects.

So what does this unlock

Tacit knowledge turns into a reproducible standard, speeding up new-inspector training, and quality variance during night and shift work drops, lowering claim risk.

04
Demand Forecasting · Inventory Distribution · Logistics
Basis for order decisions An individual staffer's gut feeling

Inventory once managed by gut feeling, cutting stockouts and dead stock at the same time

When order quantity per SKU is decided by a staffer's experience and gut feel, two things happen at once: fast-selling products run out and lose sales opportunities, while out-of-season products pile up in the warehouse as dead stock. Both losses come from the same root cause, a lack of quantitative grounding. And when the person in charge changes, that intuition disappears entirely.

Insight

The inventory problem isn't 'order more or less,' it's failing to distinguish which SKUs are stockout-prone and which are dead-stock-prone. Once you separate the two, you can prescribe opposite treatments for each.

2i's Approach

We build a time-series demand forecasting model that combines sales history with external signals like seasonal index, promotions, and weather, and automatically convert the forecast into a recommended order list with a 'suggested order quantity + rationale' attached. It doesn't replace the staffer's intuition, it's a support tool that catches the SKUs intuition misses.

  1. Measure GateWe separate stockout-prone from dead-stock-prone SKUs using POS data, order history, turnover, and stockout logs, and confirm the staffer's actual ordering criteria through interviews.
  2. Verify GateWe apply the forecasting model to 20-30 core SKUs, run it alongside the existing method for 4 weeks, and measure the improvement in forecast error, stockouts, and overstock.
  3. Operate GateWe extend the verified model to all SKUs and hand over a daily order-recommendation dashboard along with an automatic retraining routine driven by sales results.

Dead (long-idle) stockAccumulating→About 20% reduction (target)

AI Support Agent Service · Online Retail
Share of routine, repetitive inquiries Half+ of support volume

Let AI handle the repeat questions, so people can focus on real conversations

Routine inquiries like order status, shipping, refunds, and stock often make up more than half of a support channel's volume. When a small team has to handle all of it, the inquiries that actually need judgment, like claims mediation or new-customer handling, get pushed back in the queue, and response quality drops across the board. At night and on weekends, inquiries just sit there and customers wait until the next business day.

Insight

The success of an AI chatbot isn't about 'how smart it is,' it's about how clearly you draw the line between what the AI handles and what gets handed off to a person. Never automating high-value refunds or emotionally charged claims is precisely what builds trust.

2i's Approach

A support agent integrated with your order, inventory, and CS systems handles routine inquiries, and when it judges a case beyond its scope, it automatically hands off to a human agent along with the full conversation context. The core of the design is nailing down the 'handoff boundary' as an explicit rule. It's not about replacing people, it's about redeploying them to focus on what matters.

  1. Measure Gate · 2 weeksWe classify 4 weeks of inquiry logs by type, quantify the share of routine inquiries and average handling time, and finalize the boundary between what can be automated and the exceptions that must always go to a human (high-value refunds, claims).
  2. Verify Gate · 4 weeksWe limit exposure to three scenarios, order lookup, shipment tracking, and basic refund guidance, on a small channel, with agents reviewing afterward, and measure accuracy, handoff rate, and satisfaction.
  3. Operate Gate · 6 weeksWe extend the verified scenarios to all channels and hand over an error-log and handoff-reason dashboard so the operations team can tune it themselves.

So what does this unlock

The same headcount can now handle more high-difficulty cases, fewer customers are lost overnight or on weekends, and agents return to building relationships instead of being 'typing machines.'

06
Signal Fingerprinting · Telecom Smart Factory · Wireless Infrastructure
Interference-source identification Unidentified every time · temporary fixes

Pinpointing, in real time, the 'unidentified radio signal' that keeps stopping your wireless network

A factory's wireless sensor network, AGVs, and RFID keep dropping out from unidentified radio interference, stopping the line, but no one can pin down whether the cause is a microwave, nearby equipment, or an outside unlicensed transmitter, so every incident just gets a temporary fix. The few minutes the line is down are pure production loss, and because the cause is unknown, it keeps happening again.

Insight

The conventional wisdom for identifying an interference source is 'collect a mountain of training data and train a classifier,' but that kind of labeled data doesn't exist on the factory floor. Matching a signal's 'fingerprint' (its occupancy pattern) in real time, without training, lets you pinpoint the cause without ever building a dataset.

2i's Approach

We use our sigprint engine, which captures anomalous signals in real time with CFAR detection, registers the waveforms of candidate interference sources in a signature DB, and pinpoints the cause through robust-hash fingerprint matching. It's designed for millisecond-level processing and operation in weak-signal environments. The same fingerprinting approach can also extend to acoustic and vibration anomaly detection for equipment. On our own benchmark (WiSig, same-session multi-packet fusion / circular condition), the same engine measured an open-set identification AUROC of 0.945, correctly rejecting signal sources it had never seen. We're upfront that this figure is a research-condition result, and that under real product conditions, cross-day and 1:N-attacker settings, TAR@FAR of 1e-3 drops to roughly 0.22-0.43.

  1. Measure GateWe compile wireless network failure and equipment stoppage history by time segment, collect on-site RF spectrum data, confirm the initial pattern of when and on which band interference recurs, and narrow the suspect equipment down to a candidate pool.
  2. Verify GateWe pilot-install the sigprint engine on an on-site gateway, capture anomalous signals with CFAR, register candidate waveforms in the signature DB, and pinpoint the actual cause via robust-hash matching. We also collect acoustic and vibration data from key equipment to validate extensibility.
  3. Operate GateWe integrate the verified signature DB and decision logic into the operating system so that candidate causes are automatically surfaced as alerts whenever interference occurs, and hand over a staff dashboard and a response manual.
Target metrics · actual figures re-measured on your data
Interference-source IDImpossible · repeated temporary fixes→Real-time root-cause pinpointing (target)

So what does this unlock

'A stoppage with no known cause' becomes 'a cause you can point to,' cutting the time to respond to recurrence, and the same fingerprinting engine extends into predictive maintenance, catching sudden failures before they happen. This technology is 2i's own in-house signal-processing engine.

Knowledge Capture (RAG) Manufacturing · Professional Services
Where core know-how lives In the memory of 1-2 veterans

Pulling a 20-year veteran's know-how out with a single question

At many small and mid-sized manufacturers, one or two veterans with 20-plus years of tenure are effectively the sole memory for equipment troubleshooting, drawing-revision history, and client-specific quirks. Even where manuals exist, versions get mixed up or survive only as scanned copies, so in practice the work only moves when you ask that person directly. When they're out, their successor has to relearn the same problem from scratch.

Insight

The trap in knowledge capture is trying to 'load in every document.' The right answer is to start with the one document set people ask about most often, equipment troubleshooting history, drawing revision logs, and build a search system people can trust by always attaching the original source to every answer.

2i's Approach

We index manuals, drawings, and history documents into a vector-searchable form and build a RAG (retrieval-augmented generation) system that answers natural-language questions with the original source attached. Scanned and unstructured documents are converted to text via OCR and parsing. This doesn't replace the veteran, it replicates their answers into an in-house assistant you can query anytime.

  1. Measure GateWe catalog tacit knowledge through in-depth interviews with veterans and on-site shadowing, and audit the format, duplication, and gaps across scattered manuals, drawings, and history documents to lock in the priority document set to capture first.
  2. Verify GateWe build a small-scale RAG on one or two priority document sets, verify answer accuracy and source citation with 20-30 real on-site questions, and refine the glossary based on staff feedback.
  3. Operate GateWe extend the verified structure to the full document set, add an internal chat interface, department-level permissions, and a pipeline that automatically ingests new documents, and integrate it into onboarding and handover checklists.

Sales Intelligence B2B · Industrial Supply Distribution
Signal → first contact Days of delay · golden window missed

A sales radar that never misses the golden window for a proposal

Sales reps spend every morning individually searching industry news, public tender notices, and client expansion/personnel/earnings updates, and in the process miss the golden window when they should be reaching out first. What they find only lives in their personal browser history and notes, never makes it into the CRM, and disappears entirely when the rep changes.

Insight

The problem with sales signals isn't 'not enough information,' it's that even when a signal is caught, it doesn't connect to action, contact and proposal, and just dies there. The key isn't automating collection, it's automatically dropping each signal into the CRM timeline so it connects to action.

2i's Approach

We automatically collect and filter news and public data (Korea's public procurement portal, Nara Jangteo) around key clients and industry keywords, classify importance with an LLM, and route it into Slack alerts and CRM notes. It makes signals accumulate in the system, not in a rep's memory.

  1. Measure GateWe map the sales team's information-gathering routes and how CRM entry actually happens through job shadowing, and identify exactly where signals stop connecting to action.
  2. Verify GateWe run a 4-week pilot that automatically collects and filters news and notices for 20-30 key clients and industry keywords, routing them into Slack alerts and CRM notes, and reduce false positives and omissions based on feedback.
  3. Operate GateWe connect the verified filter and alerts to CRM custom fields so signals are automatically logged on the lead and client timeline, add importance scoring and automatic rep assignment, and move it into operation.
