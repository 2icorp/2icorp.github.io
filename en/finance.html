Financial Data in 7 Stages · DATA-TO-DECISION

From market prices to retrospective, all seven stages are tools we use every day.

Financial automation isn't predictive magic. It's plumbing work. We run the seven stages that turn data into judgment every day on operations where our own money is on the line, so we know exactly where it leaks. It's the same even if you're not a financial company. The same architecture applies to any company managing cash, bonds, or currency risk.

  1. 01

    Collect MARKET DATA

    Domestic and overseas prices, electronic disclosures (DART), supply-demand flow, news, macro indicators. We automatically collect scattered sources and verify when each was actually published. The first rule: never call stale data "today's" without labeling it.

    Prices·flowDisclosuresNews·macro
  2. 02

    Normalize & Store NORMALIZE

    Different ticker systems and time zones across sources get unified into one canonical time series. We enforce point-in-time consistency in code so a backtest can never peek into the future (look-ahead bias).

    Canonical time seriesPoint-in-time consistency
  3. 03

    Research BACKTEST

    We confirm whether a rule actually worked with a backtest, not a hunch. Conservative assumptions that include fees and slippage are the default, and a validation step that questions overfitting is built in.

    Backtest engineCost-inclusive assumptionsRun on our own operations
  4. 04

    Risk RISK

    Volatility, correlation, scenarios, option Greeks. We make sure the system notices before a person does, the moment a position moves outside what it can absorb.

    Volatility·correlationOption GreeksScenarios
  5. 05

    Decision Support DECIDE · HUMAN GATE

    The morning briefing, condition-triggered alerts, and a reasoned proposal are as far as the system's job goes. A person makes the decision. This approval gate isn't a shortfall in our financial automation. It's the differentiator. We don't build unapproved automatic execution.

    Morning briefingConditional alertsApproval gateRun on our own operations
  6. 06

    Execution Integration EXECUTE

    An approved decision executes through the brokerage API, and every order gets logged to a journal. Risk limits and a kill switch are hard-coded, so the system stops even when a person makes a mistake.

    Brokerage APIOrder journalKill switch
  7. 07

    Review REVIEW

    Execution records and the reasoning behind each decision turn into a retrospective report automatically. Telling apart a trade that followed the rules but lost money from one that broke the rules but made money, that's where a real financial data culture begins.

    Automated retrospectiveProcess scoringRun on our own operations
