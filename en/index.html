Honesty in measurement

We publish the results where we lost, too.

A company that sells measurement has to hold its own measurements to the same standard. Below are results we published as they are, even where they don't favor 2i. We think this tells you more than a portfolio of only the wins would.

We pointed our own diagnostic tool at ourselves, and lost.

We ran our own storage diagnostic kit against our own storage. It scored 41.7 with 15.4% coverage, behind MinIO's 72.7 and 28.2%, our comparison target. We published it as a paper instead of deleting it.

 The same model dropped from 0.931 to 0.088.

We re-measured a WiFi sensing model by changing only how the data was split. Accuracy that was 0.931 under a random split fell to 0.088, essentially a coin flip, under a per-person split. A lot of industry numbers rest on exactly this trap.

 Three replicas is not the same thing as durability.

Turn off checksums and bit-rot detection drops to 0%. Turn off background scrubbing and 13.3% of objects vanish within 6 rounds. We published the real limits of a commonly used triple-replication setup in a reproducible form.

The age of robotics & automation

Money flows to automated factories. The problem is readiness.

AI is moving past chatbots into Physical AI, where it connects to sensors, equipment, and robots, and capital and government budgets are flowing there. But bring in a robot arm without the data, eyes, signals, and judgment to drive it, and it becomes an expensive decoration. Most mid-market manufacturers aren't ready for this yet, which is exactly why now is the time to get in. 2i owns the software brain layer that makes automation actually pay off.
